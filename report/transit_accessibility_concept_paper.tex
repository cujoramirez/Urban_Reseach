\textbf{\color{pijakblue}Abstract}\par\smallskip
\small
Rapid-transit expansion in Greater Jakarta moves people along trunk lines, but the first and last
kilometre is walked, and mainstream pedestrian routing treats it as a distance-minimisation
problem. This concept paper consolidates what the team has established along the Sudirman
corridor and at Dukuh Atas, and proposes what to build and test next. The documented evidence is
limited and is labelled as such: a one-day, daytime intercept study (18~June 2026; 16~people)
in which interviewees praised the main footway but raised heat, kerb-to-crossing drops, motorbike
taxis on the footway, and night isolation at specific nodes; an OpenStreetMap and Jakarta~Satu
pedestrian network and facility layer in which accessibility attributes are almost entirely
unrecorded (no mapped bench, accessible toilet or tactile paving at Dukuh Atas; no width, surface or
kerb-ramp value on any of 548 network edges); and a source-derived sidewalk-tag screening of 68
street groups, which indicates where to verify rather than where sidewalks are absent. We then
propose a four-pillar routing model (fastest, comfort, cheapest, step-free) with an explicit rule
that \emph{unknown is not passable}; a remote-sensing pipeline (Tile2Net and shade modelling); and
a decision framework that reserves in-situ work for interchange choke points, step-free
verification with wheelchair users, and low-confidence model output. No results of the proposed
engine or pipeline are reported, and every threshold without a cited source is a provisional
design parameter.
\end{tcolorbox}

\smallskip
\noindent{\small\textbf{\color{pijakblue}Keywords:} pedestrian accessibility, step-free routing,
multimodal transit, thermal comfort, OpenStreetMap, Tile2Net, participatory field research, Jakarta.}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}
% =========================================================================

\subsection{Context and motivation}

Jalan Jenderal Sudirman is Jakarta's showcase for pedestrian revitalisation. Under the 2017--2022
programme of Dinas Bina Marga, the Sudirman--Thamrin footway was rebuilt to a reported width of
8--12~m \cite{beritajakarta2019}. Studies of the revitalised corridor report acceptable to high
walkability: a combined PEQI of 74.49 for the fourth Sudirman segment, with a notable gap between
perception (84.70) and physical condition (64.28) \cite{dwisadana2024}, and Global Walkability Index
values of 84--89 at five survey locations, against about 64 for Jalan Sudirman in 2015
\cite{mulyadi2022,ridhani2015}. The corridor sits in a city where sidewalk provision is thin: ITDP
reports 540~km of sidewalk against 6,956~km of road in 2015 (about 7.8\%) \cite{itdp2019}, and
walking and cycling together make up under 2\% of Jabodetabek commute trips (BPS Komuter 2023, as
reported by \cite{goodstats2024}). Dukuh Atas, the northern anchor of the corridor, has been studied
as a transit-oriented-development walkability case \cite{rakhmatulloh2020,napitupulu2025tod}.

For people with a permanent mobility limitation, a wide footway is necessary but not sufficient.
Whether a trip succeeds depends on steps, the presence and location of lifts, ramp gradients,
accessible toilets and places to rest---attributes that general-purpose maps rarely record. The
team's caregiver interview and accessibility mentoring (26~June 2026) described plans that failed on
arrival: a station lift far from the platform, a building that looks accessible but has no ramp at its
main entrance, and no way to check in advance short of surveying the place in person. These
accounts rest on one caregiver and one expert, with no wheelchair user interviewed yet; they are
treated here as a direction to validate, not as proof.

This paper therefore asks two open questions. \emph{What would a routing and facility-information
layer need to know to support independent, comfortable and affordable multimodal trips from
Sudirman's transit nodes?} And \emph{where can that knowledge come from at scale, and where must
people go and check?}

Three lessons shaped the proposal.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{The main footway was not what interviewees criticised (Phase 1).} Respondents on the
    Sudirman footway mostly described it favourably (words used include \emph{enak}, \emph{lega} and
    \emph{manusiawi}), and the planning critic (R11) was recorded as saying the footway is already
    good and the problem is climate fit. The concerns raised were
    heat (feels-like up to 35\,$^\circ$C), the drop between footway and zebra crossing (R05, R10),
    ojol riding onto the footway (R12), and isolation after about 22:00 at the JPO and the parking
    area (R07, R09). These are daytime interviews on the main footway; night, women, rain and the
    back-streets were not covered.
  \item \textbf{Facilities before routes (Phase 2).} The mentor's argument was that routes are too
    dynamic while facilities are permanent, so the team chose to map which places are accessible
    first and to demote routing to a recommendation (decision of 28~June 2026).
  \item \textbf{Manual audit does not scale (rationale for Phase 3).} Field Day~1 covered one day
    with two teams. The Bundaran~HI--Semanggi network alone contains 548 mapped linear edges, none of
    which carries a width, surface or kerb-ramp value (Section~\ref{sec:network}). Verifying
    everything by hand is infeasible; verifying everything by model is unsafe. Hence a split
    between remote sensing and targeted fieldwork.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Scope and how to read the evidence}\label{sec:scope}

\textbf{Scope.} The evidence in this paper comes from the Sudirman corridor and the Dukuh Atas
interchange, referenced by named streets. ``Greater Jakarta'' is the intended application scope of
the routing concept, not an area that has been studied.

\begin{evidencebox}{Three evidence classes}
Every data subsection is tagged with one of three classes so that findings, source gaps and designs
are never confused.
\begin{itemize}[itemsep=0.25em]
  \item \textbf{\textcolor{pijakgreen}{Field-observed.}} Collected by the team in the field on
    18~June 2026. Security and comfort scores are \emph{analyst-interpreted} 1--10 scales from
    qualitative transcripts, not measurements, and no inferential statistics are applied to them.
  \item \textbf{\textcolor{pijakamber}{Source-derived.}} Computed from OpenStreetMap or Jakarta~Satu.
    Absence of a feature in a source is a \emph{coverage gap}, not proof of absence on the ground.
  \item \textbf{\textcolor{pijakblue}{Proposed design.}} Models, pipelines and protocols. Thresholds
    without a cited source are provisional parameters to be calibrated, not findings.
\end{itemize}
\end{evidencebox}

\subsection{Contributions}

\begin{enumerate}
  \item A consolidated, traceable summary of the evidence gathered so far, with its limits stated
    (Section~\ref{sec:evidence}, Appendix~\ref{app:register}).
  \item A corrected reading of the per-street sidewalk-tag screening, framed as a prioritisation tool
    for field verification (Section~\ref{sec:gradient}).
  \item A four-pillar routing model with an explicit unknown-is-not-passable rule and a register of
    provisional parameters (Section~\ref{sec:model}).
  \item A proposed spatial-computing pipeline and a decision framework that allocates field research
    by rule rather than by habit (Sections~\ref{sec:pipeline} and~\ref{sec:field}).
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Facility layer at Dukuh Atas}

\evclass{pijakamber}{source-derived}
Within the Dukuh Atas bounding box ($106.817^\circ$--$106.828^\circ$E, $6.197^\circ$--$6.208^\circ$S),
OpenStreetMap yields 235 features: 114 pedestrian paths, 56 sidewalks, 58 crossings, 4 lifts,
2 drinking-water points and 1 toilet.

\begin{conceptbox}{The accessibility gap in OpenStreetMap}
In the same bounding box OpenStreetMap holds \textbf{no record of a bench, a wheelchair-accessible
toilet or tactile paving}. This is a mapping gap and says nothing about what exists on the ground:
the survey is what closes it. The larger Bundaran~HI--Semanggi network in Section~\ref{sec:network}
uses a different bounding box and does contain one mapped bench and four toilets.
\end{conceptbox}

\section{Why Distance-Minimising Routing Falls Short}\label{sec:scenarios}
% =========================================================================

\begin{evidencebox}{Evidence status of the scenario figures}
The scenario figures below are single-run observations recorded in the Pijak prototype's
head-to-head view. They were not archived with screenshots, timestamps, device and app version, and
the prototype's own figures are not fully consistent with one another (for example a stated fare
saving against a stated taxi fare). They illustrate failure modes to be tested. They are not measured
performance and must be re-run and archived before being cited.
\end{evidencebox}

\subsection{Design premise}

A standard pedestrian router minimises a single static objective,
\begin{equation}
  \min_{P}\ \sum_{e\in P}\frac{\ell(e)}{v_{w}},
  \label{eq:standard}
\end{equation}
where $\ell(e)$ is edge length and $v_w$ a constant walking speed. Equation~\eqref{eq:standard} has no
term for heat, darkness, steps, fares or the choice of mode. Where those matter, it can return a
route that is shortest and still unusable.

\subsection{Worked scenario: a late-evening trip from BSD}

The prototype's head-to-head scenario is a 22:30 departure from the Apple Developer Academy @ BINUS
(BSD, Green Office Park~9) to Jl.\ Kuburan Lama, Palmerah/Kebayoran. As recorded in the prototype:

\begin{itemize}
  \item The mainstream-engine run offered a transit option of about 1\,h\,48\,min, with travel times
    elsewhere in the run ranging from 3\,h\,32\,min to 7\,h\,50\,min; the longest was a detour of about
    38\,km towards the Kalideres/airport perimeter.
  \item One walking leg of 47 minutes ran through unlit back-alleys with open drains (\emph{gang
    tikus}, \emph{got}).
  \item The alternative to transit was a taxi at a fare in the order of Rp~80,000--90,000 or more.
  \item The prototype's chained route combined a 6-minute feeder (shuttle or ojol) to Stasiun Rawa
    Buntu, a 26-minute KRL ride (Rp~3,000 fare) and an 8-minute walk along a lit commercial frontage:
    40 minutes in total.
\end{itemize}

The last item is a demonstration output of the prototype, not an evaluation. What the scenario
illustrates is structural: a router that sees only one mode at a time cannot chain a feeder to a
trunk line, and a router without a lighting layer cannot tell a lit frontage from an unlit alley.

\section{The Four-Pillar Routing Model}\label{sec:model}
% =========================================================================

\evclass{pijakblue}{proposed design}
We formulate routing on a directed graph $G=(V,E)$ whose edge set is partitioned into walking
footways $E_w$, scheduled transit links $E_t$ and on-demand feeder connectors $E_m$ (ojol and
shuttles).

\subsection{Attribute states and the unknown-is-not-passable rule}

Each edge attribute takes either a known value or the state $\bot$ (unknown). Section~\ref{sec:network}
shows that every comfort and step-free attribute of every current edge is $\bot$. The model therefore
distinguishes two modes:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Strict mode} (step-free): an edge is admissible only if every required attribute is
    \emph{known} and satisfies its threshold. Unknown is treated as impassable.
  \item \textbf{Advisory mode} (all other pillars): unknown attributes take a conservative default,
    and the route is labelled \emph{modelled}, never \emph{verified}.
\end{itemize}
A route may be labelled \emph{verified} only when field evidence exists for every edge it depends on.

\subsection{Pillar 1: Fastest (multimodal chaining)}

\begin{equation}
  \min_{P}\ T(P)=\sum_{e\in P\cap E_w}\frac{\ell(e)}{v_w}
   +\sum_{e\in P\cap E_t}t_t(e)+\sum_{e\in P\cap E_m}t_m(e)+\sum_{k\in K(P)}\tau_k,
  \label{eq:fastest}
\end{equation}
where $t_t(e)$ is in-vehicle time plus expected wait, $t_m(e)$ the feeder trip time including
pick-up wait, $K(P)$ the set of transfers on $P$, and $\tau_k$ a transfer penalty covering the walk
inside the station, fare-gate clearance and the headway of the next service.

\subsection{Pillar 2: Comfort (shade, night safety and walk cap)}

The comfort cost of a walking edge $e\in E_w$ at time $t$ is
\begin{equation}
  C_{\mathrm{comf}}(e,t)=\ell(e)\Big[1+w_s\big(1-S_e(t)\big)+w_l\,\frac{2-L_e}{2}
   +w_v\,\frac{2-V_e}{2}+w_d\,D_e\Big],
  \label{eq:comfort}
\end{equation}
where
\begin{itemize}
  \item $S_e(t)\in[0,1]$ is the shaded fraction of the edge (Section~\ref{sec:shade});
  \item $L_e\in\{0,1,2\}$ is lighting (unlit, partial or blocked by foliage, continuous);
  \item $V_e\in\{0,1,2\}$ is natural surveillance (blank frontage to active frontage), so that the
    ``lit but isolated'' pattern reported at the JPO (R09) can be represented;
  \item $D_e\in\{0,1\}$ flags a damaged surface or open drain.
\end{itemize}
The weights $w_s$ apply while the sun is up and $w_l,w_v$ after dusk. A walking-distance cap
\begin{equation}
  \sum_{e\in P\cap E_w}\ell(e)\le D_{\max}(T_a),\qquad
  D_{\max}(T_a)=\begin{cases}D_0,& T_a\le T^{*}\\ \rho\,D_0,& T_a>T^{*}\end{cases}\quad(0<\rho<1)
  \label{eq:cap}
\end{equation}
limits exposure. The baseline $D_0$ is anchored to stated acceptable walking distances around
Jakarta MRT stations---547\,m near residential areas to 689\,m near offices---\cite{afkara2020walking}.
The heat contraction $(\rho,T^{*})$ has no source-backed value yet and is calibrated from field data.

\subsection{Pillar 3: Cheapest (Rupiah tariff engine)}

\begin{equation}
  \min_{P}\ \mathrm{Fare}(P)\quad\text{subject to}\quad T(P)\le\alpha\,T_{\mathrm{fastest}},\ \alpha\ge1,
  \label{eq:cheapest}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
  \mathrm{Fare}(P)=\sum_{r\in R_{\mathrm{KRL}}(P)}\Big(3000+1000\Big\lceil\tfrac{\max(0,\,d_r-25)}{10}\Big\rceil\Big)
   +3500\,\lvert R_{\mathrm{TJ}}(P)\rvert+\sum_{e\in P\cap E_m}f_m(e),
  \label{eq:fare}
\end{equation}
with $d_r$ the ride distance in km, $R_{\mathrm{TJ}}(P)$ the TransJakarta boardings that are not
free transfers, and $f_m(e)$ an estimated feeder fare. JakLingko Mikrotrans contributes Rp~0.
These are public tariffs at the time of writing; they are not part of the desk-research set,
change by policy, and must be re-verified at implementation. MRT Jakarta and LRT Jabodebek fares are
distance-based and not yet modelled, although the proposed co-testing route uses MRT.

\subsection{Pillar 4: Step-free (strict admissibility)}

For a permanent wheelchair user, comfort is not continuous: one unramped step ends the journey. An
edge is \emph{admissible} only if every predicate below is known to hold,
\begin{equation}
  \begin{aligned}
  \mathrm{Adm}(e)\iff{}&\big(\mathrm{stairs}(e)=\text{no}\ \lor\ \mathrm{lift}(e)=\text{operational}\big)
    \ \land\ \mathrm{slope}(e)\le s_{\max}\ \land\ \mathrm{width}(e)\ge w_{\min}\\
    &\land\ \mathrm{surface}(e)\ne\text{damaged}\ \land\ \lnot\,\mathrm{open\_drain}(e),
  \end{aligned}
  \label{eq:admissible}
\end{equation}
where a predicate evaluating to $\bot$ counts as not satisfied, and every kerb transition on the edge
must be ramped. The step-free cost is then
\begin{equation}
  C_{\mathrm{wc}}(e)=\begin{cases}
    \infty, & \lnot\,\mathrm{Adm}(e),\\[0.2em]
    \ell(e)\big(1+\mu\,\mathrm{slope}(e)\big), & \text{otherwise.}
  \end{cases}
  \label{eq:wheelchair}
\end{equation}
The slope penalty $\mu$ is to be calibrated from user-rated effort in the co-testing sessions
(Section~\ref{sec:cotest}).

\subsection{Provenance and conflation}

Every feature carries its source and a confidence value, and sources are merged with a fixed
precedence: field audit, then OpenStreetMap, then Jakarta Satu, then model output. Missing values
remain \texttt{unknown}; a segment without geometry is dropped rather than given a guessed line.
This rule is what allows the model of Section~\ref{sec:model} to separate \emph{verified} from
\emph{modelled}.

\subsection{Aerial segmentation with Tile2Net}

Tile2Net segments orthorectified aerial tiles into roadway, sidewalk, crosswalk and footpath classes,
vectorises the polygons and derives a topologically connected centreline network \cite{tile2net2023}.
It was trained on North American cities, so validation and fine-tuning on Jakarta imagery are
required, where roof types, tree canopy and informal street layouts differ. Sub-metre aerial tiles
are needed, their source and licence must be checked, and tiles are kept local-only and never
redistributed. Reporting Jakarta accuracy against the team's own field audit would itself be a
contribution.

\subsection{Street-level imagery}

Street-level frames (for example from Mapillary) are a candidate source for kerb ramps, tactile
paving and obstructions. They are not yet evaluated, and the choice of model is open.

\subsection{Shade layer}\label{sec:shade}

CoolWalks adds shade preference to pedestrian routing using building footprints, building heights
and sun position, and reports that its benefit depends on street geometry and is sensitive to the
precision of the mapped network \cite{coolwalks2025}. The team's implementation plan follows the same
inputs. For solar zenith angle $\theta_z$ and azimuth $\alpha_s$, a building of height $H$ casts a
shadow in direction $\alpha_s+180^\circ$ of length
\begin{equation}
  L_{\mathrm{shadow}}=H\tan\theta_z=\frac{H}{\tan h_s},\qquad h_s=90^\circ-\theta_z,
  \label{eq:shadow}
\end{equation}
and exists only while $\theta_z<90^\circ$. The shadow of a building is the union of its footprint, its
footprint translated by $L_{\mathrm{shadow}}$, and the quadrilaterals swept by each footprint edge.
With $\Omega(t)$ the union of all building shadows, the shaded fraction of an edge is
\begin{equation}
  S_e(t)=\frac{\lvert e\cap\Omega(t)\rvert}{\lvert e\rvert}\in[0,1].
  \label{eq:shaded-fraction}
\end{equation}
Heights are derived from the number of building levels at a provisional 3.2\,m per level. A
buildings-only layer cannot see tree canopy or covered walkways, which respondents explicitly credited
with cooling (R02); it will therefore under-estimate shade on tree-lined sections. The layer must be
validated against on-site observation before it is used for routing.

\section{How and When to Do Field Research}\label{sec:field}
% =========================================================================

\evclass{pijakblue}{proposed design}
Two failure modes bracket the design. \emph{Automation arrogance} assumes remote sensing can replace
physical inspection; \emph{manual audit fatigue} tries to inspect every metre by hand.

\begin{conceptbox}{Allocation rule for field research}
Remote sensing offers coverage; field research offers verification. Fieldwork is expensive, so it is
reserved for safety-critical choke points, step-free claims that have not been verified, and edges
where the model is not confident enough---and everything else is accepted only as \emph{modelled}.
\end{conceptbox}

\subsection{Participatory co-testing with wheelchair users}\label{sec:cotest}

The prototype was informed by a caregiver and an accessibility mentor but not by people who use
wheelchairs themselves; reaching them is the open validation gap. The team intends to approach
disability-led and community organisations, for example Wisma Cheshire Indonesia (a residential
centre for people with physical disabilities in Cilandak, South Jakarta) and Jakarta Barrier Free
Tourism. \textbf{No partnership is confirmed}; the protocol below is a proposal.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Independence as the design goal.} The expert interview stressed that users want to
    travel independently rather than be told not to go alone because a helper is available, and
    that being carried by staff undermines this. Verified step-free information is meant to serve that aim, consistent with the
    accessibility and independence principles of the UN CRPD Article~9 \cite{uncrpd2006}.
  \item \textbf{Walk-along sessions.} With 3--5 manual wheelchair users, test a transit chain such as
    Cilandak $\rightarrow$ MRT Fatmawati $\rightarrow$ MRT Dukuh Atas $\rightarrow$ destination, logging
    where a single missing ramp or out-of-service lift breaks the chain.
  \item \textbf{Calibrating $\mu$.} Candidate measures are self-rated effort, completion time, push
    count and assistance events. Physiological measures such as heart rate are used only with
    informed consent and appropriate oversight.
  \item \textbf{Safeguards.} Informed consent in Bahasa Indonesia; the right to stop at any time; a
    companion present; routes reconnoitred beforehand; no participant asked to attempt a drop or slope
    they judge unsafe; no identifiable images without consent; minimal personal data; results
    shared back with participants and partners.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Implementation Notes}\label{sec:impl}
% =========================================================================

\evclass{pijakblue}{proposed design}
Product and stack decisions are recorded in the team's architecture notes; only what bears on the
research is repeated here.

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Data and client are decoupled.} The spatial pipeline is engine-agnostic and emits
    GeoJSON and a bundled GeoPackage containing precomputed shade values and accessibility metadata.
  \item \textbf{Native client.} The planned client is native (Xcode, SwiftUI, MapKit), not a
    cross-platform 3D runtime. A native map inherits VoiceOver and Dynamic Type, which matters for an
    accessibility product.
  \item \textbf{Place recommendation.} The prototype ranks places with a weighted score of
    accessibility-facility completeness, proximity and quality (provisional weights
    0.65/0.20/0.15, Table~\ref{tab:params}). The weights are uncalibrated and need a sensitivity
    analysis before any claim rests on them.
  \item \textbf{Place-data quality filters (proposed).} Because the prototype fuses crowd-sourced
    place data, three defensive filters are planned: (i) flag clusters of several commercial places
    sharing identical coordinates to five decimal places; (ii) a string sanitiser for promotional
    text, telephone numbers and gambling keywords; and (iii) reject a match between two source pins
    farther apart than a maximum distance (initially 100\,m, combined with fuzzy name matching,
    to be tuned).
\end{itemize}
