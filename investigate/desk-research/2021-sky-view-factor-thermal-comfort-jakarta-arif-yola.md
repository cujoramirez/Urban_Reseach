# Sky View Factor & pedestrian thermal comfort — a Jakarta sidewalk

## Citation
Arif, V., & Yola, L. (2021). *The impact of sky view factor on pedestrian thermal comfort in
tropical context: A case of Jakarta sidewalk*. In Sustainable Architecture and Building
Environment (Lecture Notes in Civil Engineering, pp. 27–33). Springer.
https://doi.org/10.1007/978-981-16-2329-5_4
(Springer Nature · **access-restricted** — abstract not openly available)

## Key findings
> ⚠️ **Access note:** the full text is paywalled and no abstract is openly indexed. The points
> below describe the paper's **topic and the established relationship it investigates** — they
> are **not** specific results lifted from this paper. **Confirm the actual findings/figures
> from the full chapter** before citing them.

- Investigates how **Sky View Factor (SVF)** — how open vs. shaded the sky is above a footpath —
  relates to **pedestrian thermal comfort** on a **Jakarta sidewalk**, in a hot-humid tropical
  setting.
- Sits in a well-established literature where **less shade / more sky exposure generally means
  greater heat stress** for pedestrians; tree canopy and built shading typically improve
  outdoor thermal comfort. *(General background, not a claim about this paper's numbers.)*

## How it relates to our challenge
"Comfort" is the provisional word at the centre of our challenge, and **shade** is parameter #9
on our audit sheet. This paper anchors *heat and shade* as a measurable comfort driver on a
Jakarta footpath specifically — useful background for both our **audit** (shade scoring) and our
**intercepts** (asking whether heat/sun shapes where and when people walk).
