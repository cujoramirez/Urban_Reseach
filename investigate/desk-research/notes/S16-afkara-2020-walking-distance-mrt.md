# Walking-distance perception around Jakarta MRT stations

> **S16 · CORRIDOR-relevant (MRT last-mile) · walking-distance thresholds.** Jakarta-specific: ~629 m men / 593 m women; 689 m office / 547 m residential. Localizes the generic TOD 400/800 m benchmark (cf. CAP-12, S6). _(From colleague branch.)_

## Citation
Afkara, A. V., & Kusuma, A. (2020). *Walking distance perception in Jakarta MRT station area*.
In Proceedings of the 2nd International Symposium on Transportation Studies in Developing
Countries (ISTSDC 2019) (Advances in Engineering Research). Atlantis Press.
https://doi.org/10.2991/aer.k.200220.025
(Atlantis Press · Gold open access)

## Key findings
- Examines how willing people are to **walk to/from a Jakarta MRT station** versus taking BRT
  (Transjakarta) or **ojek/motorcycle taxi** for the first/last leg of a trip.
- Uses a **stated-preference survey** analysed with a **binomial logit (discrete choice)** model.
- Reports walking-distance thresholds (as stated by the authors): compared with other modes,
  the perceived acceptable walking distance to MRT was roughly **629 m for men and 593 m for
  women**, and around **689 m near office areas vs 547 m near residential areas**.
- Implies the choice to walk is **sensitive to distance and trip context**, not fixed.

## How it relates to our challenge
The Sudirman corridor is an MRT corridor, so first/last-mile walking is part of daily life here.
This gives us **concrete distance figures** to sanity-check our own intercepts ("how far is too
far to walk?") and a reminder that walking competes with **ojol/ojek**, a behaviour our audit
and intercept should watch for, neutrally, at the kerb.

## Fieldwork prompts this suggests (neutral)
- When you came here, did you walk part of the way or use ojek/ojol/Transjakarta? What made you
  choose that? *(Neutral, does not assume walking is preferred or avoided.)*
- Roughly how far are you willing to walk before you'd rather take another mode?
