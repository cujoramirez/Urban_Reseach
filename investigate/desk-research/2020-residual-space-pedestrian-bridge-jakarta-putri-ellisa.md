# Informal occupancy of residual space beneath pedestrian bridges — Jakarta

## Citation
Putri, U., & Ellisa, E. (2020). *Reclaiming residual spaces in urban life: The act of occupancy
beneath pedestrian bridges in Jakarta*. Evergreen, 7(1), 126–131.
https://doi.org/10.5109/2740969
(Kyushu University · Diamond open access)

## Key findings
- Looks at the **"residual" / leftover spaces beneath pedestrian bridges** in Jakarta — spaces
  "owned by no one but the public" — and how they get **informally occupied and used**.
- Notes Jakarta had (per 2015 local-government data) **324 pedestrian bridges**, mostly for
  crossing highways and **mostly placed near transit areas**.
- Uses the **Lenteng Agung pedestrian bridge (South Jakarta)** as a case of unexpected, informal
  activity colonising pedestrian infrastructure.
- Frames pedestrian infrastructure as **socially contested space**, not just a clear path —
  things happen on and around it that designers didn't plan for.

## How it relates to our challenge
Our scope centres on the **gradient** between a designed main trotoar and the messy back-streets.
This paper is a reminder that **pedestrian space is used informally** — by vendors, parked
motorbikes, gatherings, shelter-seekers — which connects to our assumption #1 (sidewalks blocked
by motorbikes/vendors) and assumption #3 (people stepping into the road). It pushes us to **record
informal use neutrally as a finding**, not just as an "obstruction" to be condemned.

## Fieldwork prompts this suggests (neutral)
- When the footpath is taken up by stalls, parked motorbikes, or people, what do you do?
  *(Observe/record the actual behaviour — detour, wait, step into road, turn back.)*
- Is there anything about how people *use* these spaces that helps you, or gets in your way?
