# Transit-Oriented Development & walkability — Dukuh Atas, Jakarta

## Citation
Napitupulu, D. M. R., & Rudiarto, I. (2025). *The impact of transit-oriented development on
walkability: A case study of Dukuh Atas Station, Jakarta*. Jurnal Pembangunan Wilayah dan Kota,
21(1), 113–129. https://doi.org/10.14710/pwk.v21i1.52284
(Universitas Diponegoro · Diamond open access)

## Key findings
- Studies how **TOD intervention around Dukuh Atas** — the northern anchor of the Sudirman
  corridor and one of Jakarta's earliest TODs — affected the **walkability** of a **400 m core
  radius**.
- Opens with the point that **Jakarta has one of the world's lowest average daily step counts**,
  attributed partly to a changing urban environment and a lack of pedestrian infrastructure.
- Method is **descriptive-qualitative**: before/after maps (pre- vs post-MRT + TOD), **field
  observation**, and **pedestrian/commuter perceptions via questionnaires and interviews**.
- Treats walkability as a key indicator of how usable the built environment is for people
  walking to varied destinations for multiple purposes.

## How it relates to our challenge
Dukuh Atas is **on our corridor's northern end**, so this is near-identical terrain. Most useful
to us methodologically: it pairs **observation + maps + intercept-style questionnaires/interviews**
— the same mixed approach our audit sheet and intercept script use. It is a recent, local model
for **how to ask pedestrians about a TOD walking environment** without over-claiming.

## Fieldwork prompts this suggests (neutral)
- Since the MRT/TOD changes here, has walking around this area changed for you — better, worse,
  or about the same?
- For the kinds of trips you make, is this an easy place to get around on foot, or not?
