# User satisfaction with pathway & street-furniture facilities — Thamrin–Sudirman

## Citation
Apritasari, Y. D. (2020). *Evaluation for user's satisfaction on pathway and street furniture
facilities in Thamrin–Sudirman area*. International Journal of Built Environment and Scientific
Research, 4(1), 61–72. https://doi.org/10.24853/ijbesr.4.1.61-72
(Universitas Muhammadiyah Jakarta · Diamond open access)

## Key findings
- Evaluates **road/pedestrian users' satisfaction** with pedestrian facility requirements —
  **walkways, street furniture, and signage/nameplates** — along the **Thamrin–Sudirman**
  corridor.
- Frames the corridor as a provincial **strategic area** where DKI Jakarta has, over several
  years, built pedestrian and public-transport facilities specifically to improve user
  *convenience*.
- Samples named points strung along the corridor (e.g. around Sarinah, Bundaran HI, Dukuh Atas,
  Setiabudi, Senayan) — i.e. it treats the corridor as a sequence of distinct walking
  environments rather than one uniform space.

> ⚠️ The source abstract is partly garbled in indexing; treat the **specific satisfaction
> scores as "to verify from the full paper."** The *framing* above (what it measures and where)
> is reliable.

## How it relates to our challenge
Where Mulyadi et al. measure walkability "from above," this paper measures **satisfaction from
the user's side** on the same corridor — and singles out **street furniture and signage**,
which our audit sheet also scores. It is a direct model for the *intercept* side of our
fieldwork: asking pedestrians what does and doesn't satisfy them about the walking environment.

## Fieldwork prompts this suggests (neutral)
- Thinking about the path, benches/shelter, and signage here — what works for you, and what
  doesn't?
- Is there a spot along this corridor that feels noticeably better or worse than the rest?
