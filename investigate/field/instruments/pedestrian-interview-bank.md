# Pedestrian Interview Bank: *literature-informed* (DRAFT for team review)

A **menu of questions to draw from** when intercepting pedestrians in the Sudirman corridor,
**derived from the desk research** in [`../../desk-research/`](../../desk-research/). The point
of building it from the literature first is to ask *informed* questions, covering things prior
Jakarta studies found mattered, **without** importing their conclusions.

> **This does not replace anything.** It **complements**:
> - [`intercept-script.md`](intercept-script.md), the actual ≤5-minute on-street script (consent → warm-up → core → close).
> - [`../../../engage/guiding-questions.md`](../../../engage/guiding-questions.md), the team's neutral guiding questions.
>
> Use this as a **bank**: pick a few questions per intercept, keep it short. Final wording is a
> **team decision**, log changes in [`DECISIONS.md`](../../../DECISIONS.md).

---

## Rules of use (read before fieldwork)

1. **Stay neutral and two-sided.** Every question must allow "good," "bad," and "neither", never
   pre-load the answer. Our assumptions stay in [`assumptions.md`](../../../engage/assumptions.md),
   **out** of the questions. *(This is the exact confirmation-bias fix our mentor asked for.)*
2. **Open, in their words.** Don't suggest answers, agree/disagree, or hint at what you expect.
   Silence is fine.
3. **Consent first**, every time, use the consent script in [`intercept-script.md`](intercept-script.md).
   Ask separately before recording audio.
4. **Safety:** night and sensitive intercepts led by a female team member, with a teammate nearby.
5. These are **prompts, not a checklist to read aloud verbatim.** Adapt to the person and the moment.

## What the literature suggests we probe (the "pre-knowledge")

| Theme | Why it's worth asking | From |
|-------|-----------------------|------|
| Mode & first/last-mile choice (walk vs ojol vs Transjakarta) | Walking competes with ojek/ojol; distance willingness varies (~550–690 m) | Afkara & Kusuma 2020 |
| Distance people will walk | Concrete thresholds to sanity-check | Afkara & Kusuma 2020 |
| Facilities & satisfaction (path, street furniture, signage) | Users rate these unevenly along this corridor | Apritasari 2020; Mulyadi et al. 2022 |
| Heat & shade as comfort drivers | Sky exposure / shade shapes thermal comfort on Jakarta footpaths | Arif & Yola 2021 |
| Informal use & obstruction (vendors, parked motorbikes) | Pedestrian space is informally occupied; record behaviour, don't just judge it | Putri & Ellisa 2020 |
| The "gradient": main trotoar vs back-streets | The contrast at the centre of our scope | Mulyadi et al. 2022; team scope |
| Change around the MRT/TOD | Recent intervention may have shifted walking | Napitupulu & Rudiarto 2025 |

## Question bank (grouped: pick a few per intercept)

### 0. Consent & warm-up
Use the consent paragraph in [`intercept-script.md`](intercept-script.md), then:
- "Anda sedang dalam perjalanan ke mana, kalau boleh tahu?" *(trip purpose)*
- "Seberapa sering Anda berjalan kaki di area ini?" *(frequency)*

### A. Mode & distance  *(Afkara & Kusuma 2020)*
- "Tadi menuju ke sini, Anda berjalan kaki, naik ojek/ojol, atau Transjakarta/MRT? Apa yang
  memengaruhi pilihan itu?", *neutral; does not assume walking is preferred or avoided.*
- "Kira-kira sejauh apa Anda mau berjalan kaki sebelum memilih moda lain?"
- *Why this matters:* first/last-mile walking is central on an MRT corridor; tells us where
  walking wins or loses to ojol.

### B. The walking experience, both directions  *(team guiding Q3; Apritasari 2020)*
- "Bagaimana pengalaman Anda berjalan kaki di sini?", *open, no assumption.*
- "Apa yang membuat berjalan kaki di sini terasa **nyaman**? Apa yang membuatnya **kurang
  nyaman**?", *both directions.*
- *Why this matters:* we must not assume discomfort; let people define comfort themselves.

### C. Facilities & street furniture  *(Apritasari 2020; Mulyadi et al. 2022)*
- "Soal trotoar, tempat duduk/peneduh, dan papan petunjuk arah di sini, apa yang membantu, apa
  yang kurang?"
- "Apakah ada bagian dari koridor ini yang terasa jauh lebih baik atau lebih buruk daripada
  bagian lain?"
- *Why this matters:* prior work rates facilities unevenly along this exact corridor.

### D. Heat & shade  *(Arif & Yola 2021)*
- "Apakah panas atau sinar matahari memengaruhi **kapan** atau **di mana** Anda berjalan? Bagaimana?"
- "Ada bagian yang terasa terlalu terbuka, atau yang teduhnya pas?", *two-sided.*
- *Why this matters:* shade is audit parameter #9 and a known tropical comfort driver.

### E. Route choice: sidewalk, road, or switching  *(team guiding Q2 & Q6; Putri & Ellisa 2020)*
- "Tadi Anda berjalan di trotoar, di jalan, atau berpindah-pindah? Apa yang memengaruhi pilihan
  itu?", *neutral; does not assume road-walking.*
- "Kalau trotoar sedang terhalang, misalnya oleh PKL, motor parkir, atau orang, biasanya Anda
  melakukan apa?", *asks behaviour, not blame.*
- *Why this matters:* tests assumptions #1/#3 by observing what people actually do at obstructions.

### F. The gradient: main corridor vs back-streets  *(team scope; Mulyadi et al. 2022)*
- "Dibandingkan trotoar utama Sudirman, bagaimana rasanya berjalan di jalan-jalan satu blok di
  belakangnya?", *invites either direction.*
- *Why this matters:* the contrast is the heart of our study.

### G. Change around the MRT/TOD  *(Napitupulu & Rudiarto 2025)*: ask only if relevant to the person
- "Sejak ada MRT dan penataan di sekitar sini, apakah berjalan kaki di area ini berubah untuk
  Anda, lebih baik, lebih buruk, atau sama saja?"

### H. Close
- "Kalau ada **satu** hal yang bisa diperbaiki dari berjalan kaki di sini, apa itu?"
- "Terima kasih banyak atas waktunya!"

## ⚠️ Phrasings to avoid (same trap our mentor flagged)
- ❌ "Kenapa trotoar di sini selalu penuh motor parkir?", assumes the conclusion.
  ✅ Use **E** instead ("kalau trotoar terhalang… biasanya Anda melakukan apa?").
- ❌ "Pasti tidak nyaman jalan di sini karena panas, ya?", leads the answer.
  ✅ Use **D** ("apakah panas memengaruhi… bagaimana?").

> After fieldwork, note which questions actually worked and refine this bank. Refuted
> assumptions are **findings**, record them, don't delete them.
