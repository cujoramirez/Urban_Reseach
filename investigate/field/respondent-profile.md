# Respondent Profile: who to interview, and how to reach them

Who actually uses the corridor (and the back-streets), so we interview a *representative* mix,
not just the easy-to-reach office worker. Personas are grounded in verified sources; where the
literature is silent (the back-streets), the persona is marked **TO CHARACTERISE IN FIELD**.

> Consent and conduct: follow [`instruments/intercept-script.md`](instruments/intercept-script.md).
> Sensitive/after-dark intercepts are **female-led**. See
> [`fieldworker-presentation.md`](fieldworker-presentation.md) for how to approach without
> standing out.

---

## The personas

### P1: Office / corporate commuter (the dominant group on the main trotoar)
- **Grounding:** segment-4 pedestrians are mostly **male office workers, 19–40, normal mobility,
  Mon–Fri, commuting via transit** \[S1]; Sudirman–Thamrin sample majority **private employees
  (44%)**, high income >IDR 7 jt (44%), ages 26–35 & 36–46 \[S9]; Dukuh Atas similar \[S3].
- **Where/when:** main trotoar and transit nodes at AM/PM peaks.
- **Ask about:** comfort factors, shade, crossings, transit walk distance, why they do/don't use
  the sidewalk. (Beware: this group **tends to tolerate/accept** the sidewalk \[S1], probe past
  "it's fine.")

### P2: Women pedestrians (deliberately oversample)
- **Grounding:** women are **under-represented** in S1/S3/S9 samples, yet public streets are the
  top site of harassment nationally and women are **6× more vulnerable** \[S2]; the only named
  corridor pedestrians in recent reporting are **young women (23, 28)** \[S11].
- **Target:** aim for **≥40–50%** of intercepts to be women, across day **and** evening.
- **Ask about:** what makes walking feel comfortable/uncomfortable, safety, lighting, crowding,
  routes chosen/avoided, day vs night. (Neutral, two-sided, don't pre-load fear.)

### P3: Students / younger pedestrians
- **Grounding:** Dukuh Atas pedestrians skew **productive age 15–30** \[S3].
- **Where:** transit nodes, weekend/CFD, evenings.

### P4: Transit last-mile users (at the nodes)
- **Grounding:** flow concentrates at **Dukuh Atas / Stasiun Sudirman, GBK–FX, Ratu Plaza/Menara
  Mandiri** \[S1]; KRL riders cluster waiting for ojol and disperse on foot \[S11].
- **Where/when:** station exits, ojol waiting points, halte, JPO, AM/PM peaks.
- **Ask about:** the walk from station to destination/ojol, waiting, what would make it easier.

### P5: Informal / livelihood actors (the "obstruction" perspective, interview, don't just blame)
- **PKL / street vendors:** officially cleared from the main trotoar and **relocated to side
  streets behind the towers** in 2018 \[S12]; encroachment recurs \[S1, S10].
- **Ojol drivers / couriers:** line up on the trotoar edge at nodes \[S11].
- **Ask about:** why they use the space they use, where they were moved from/to, how enforcement
  affects them. (They are also corridor users, their account feeds the obstacle-oriented
  expert interview.)

### P6: Back-street population, **TO CHARACTERISE IN FIELD (the gap, G1)**
- **Grounding:** behind the towers are dense kampung, Karet Tengsin/Kuningan, Menteng Atas
  (dense, 11 RW), Setiabudi \[round-3 leads, *unverified*]. **No source profiles these residents
 , find out on the ground.** Likely: residents, warung workers, security/parking/cleaning staff,
  small vendors.
- **Where/when:** the paired back-street segments; daytime first; greet RT/RW.
- **Ask about:** their own walking experience, the sidewalk (or its absence), how the back-street
  compares to the main corridor for them.

### P7: Varied-mobility & care-givers
- **Grounding:** PEQI/standards emphasise ramps, guiding blocks, accessibility \[S1, S6, S9].
- **Who:** elderly, persons with visible disability, parents with children/strollers.
- **Ask about:** specific barriers (kerbs, surface, width, crossings, guiding blocks).

## Sampling frame (quota-style, non-probability: be honest about it)
Aim to fill a grid, not just rack up numbers:

| Axis | Cells to cover |
|------|----------------|
| **Location** | main trotoar · paired back-street · each transit node |
| **Persona** | P1–P7 (with P2 women oversampled, P6 back-street deliberately included) |
| **Time** | weekday AM · midday · PM · after-dark · weekend/CFD |

- Set a **per-cell minimum** the team can realistically hit (e.g. 3–5 intercepts/cell) and track
  fill on a simple tally. Stop over-sampling P1-on-main-trotoar once its cells are full.
- This is a **convenience/quota sample**, say so in the report; don't claim representativeness.

## How to recruit (intercept where people naturally pause)
People won't stop mid-stride. Approach at **natural dwell points**: waiting for ojol, at a
crossing, at a halte/JPO, resting on a bench, at a warung. Lead with the consent line, keep it
≤5 minutes, open and neutral \[intercept-script]. For P5/P6, a brief, respectful intro and
greeting local gatekeepers (RT/RW, vendor association) goes a long way.

## Cross-check against bias
If at the end the sample is 80% male office workers on the main trotoar, **we've reproduced the
literature's blind spot, not filled it.** The whole point is P2, P5, P6 and the back-streets.
